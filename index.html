MACD Field Guide

The MACD answers one question: is the trend picking up speed or running out of it? Learn its three parts, the four things to look for, then test yourself on eight charts.

What you're looking at

The MACD sits in its own pane under the price chart. Move your finger or cursor across the chart to see each value day by day.

MACD line

12-day EMA − 26-day EMA

The gap between a fast and a slow moving average. Above zero, the recent trend is up. Below zero, it's down. The further from zero, the stronger the move.

Signal line

9-day EMA of the MACD line

A slower, smoothed copy of the MACD line. It exists so you have something to compare the MACD line against.

Histogram

MACD − signal

The distance between the two lines, drawn as bars. Growing bars mean momentum is strengthening. Faded, shrinking bars mean it's weakening.

Part 2

Four things to look for

1. Crossovers

When the blue MACD line crosses above the orange signal line, momentum is turning up. Crossing below means it's turning down. The circle marks the cross.

2. The zero line

Above zero, the 12-day average is above the 26-day average, so the short-term trend is up. A cross of zero is slower than a crossover but more meaningful.

3. Histogram size

Price is still rising at the right edge, but the bars have shrunk to almost nothing. The move is losing steam. Shrinking bars usually appear before a crossover.

4. Divergence

Price made a lower low, but the MACD made a higher low. The second sell-off had less force. That's bullish divergence. The mirror image, a higher high in price with a lower high in MACD, is bearish.

Where the crossover happens matters

A crossover means more when it agrees with the zero line.

CrossoverPositionWhat it usually means
MACD crosses upAbove zeroStrongest buy signal Trend and momentum agree. An uptrend is resuming after a pause.
MACD crosses upBelow zeroEarly, weaker Selling is slowing inside a downtrend. Not yet proof buyers are in control.
MACD crosses downAbove zeroWarning An uptrend is losing momentum. Often the start of a pullback.
MACD crosses downBelow zeroStrongest sell signal Trend and momentum agree. A downtrend is resuming.

Before you rely on it

What the MACD can't tell you

  • It lags. It's built from moving averages, so it confirms a move after it has started. It will never catch the exact bottom or top.
  • It fails in sideways markets. When price chops in a range, the lines cross back and forth and most signals reverse within days.
  • Divergences can last. A strong trend can keep going for weeks while a divergence builds. Treat it as a warning, not a trigger.
  • Use it with price. Check it against the chart itself: higher lows or lower highs, support levels like the 200-day SMA, and volume. When they agree, the signal is worth more.
